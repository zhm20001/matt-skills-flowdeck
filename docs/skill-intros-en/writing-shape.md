# writing-shape

> ⚠️ In progress: this skill is not final and its behaviour may change.

**writing-shape is the shaping track of the writing workflow's exploit phase**: the raw material pile (fragments, loose walls of text, transcripts, anything) is read through once and then stays **read-only**, while a separate article file grows paragraph by paragraph. The flow: settle the premise (what the reader brings on entry) → offer 2-3 **candidate openings** (each implying a different thesis, which forces you to choose or hybridize — once the opening is set, what the rest of the article owes is set) → paragraph by paragraph ask "given this opening, what does the reader need to hear next", answer from the pile, **append a paragraph the moment it is agreed**, and loop until you declare the piece finished.

Throughout, it grills itself instead of you: what does this paragraph do beyond the previous one? What breaks if it is cut? Prose or list? The opening promised X and the draft has drifted to Y — change the line or change the opening? Format trade-offs (prose vs lists, inline vs callout boxes, tables vs repeated structure, quoting vs paraphrasing) go on the table and get argued openly; when the pile is short of something it names the gap — "give me an example now, or this paragraph gets cut". The grounding discipline comes from the same source as writing-beats.

## When to use

- The raw material is in hand and you want to shape it into a finished article.
